Level 2 · Inside the copper

Green outside. Copper inside.

Small holdings, one shared prize vault, three steps up. The ten-minute rhythm is a proposed rule, not a running timer.

  1. 01

    Bring your dust

    Hold at least $10 in $LIBERTY for your base entry. Then link up to 5 secondary wallets holding RWA dust. Sign a message to prove control — no gas, no transaction.

  2. 02

    Earn entries

    Every dust contribution over $0.50 earns 1 entry per linked wallet per cycle. One wallet, one entry — no stacking. A 2% protocol fee comes off each deposit before entry weight is calculated.

  3. 03

    Draw and share out

    Every hour, if the prize pool has at least $50, 10 unique wallets win. If the pool is below the threshold the drawing is skipped and funds roll over. Winners are excluded for 24 hours.

        Questions the pigeon asked.

        He asked them all. Loudly. On the windowsill.

        What's an RWA?

        A tokenized real-world asset: a token on a blockchain that stands for something off-chain, like a share of a company or a fund. AAPLx, for example, tracks Apple shares.

        Which assets can go in?

        AAPLx, NVDAx, TSLAx and SPYx are examples from the concept. The real list of supported tokens and their addresses isn't decided, and nothing can go in yet.

        Why is the vault empty?

        Because it doesn't exist yet. There's no deployed contract, no deposit address and no deposits. Zeros are the honest number.

        How is my entry value worked out?

        The oracle values your deposit in USD and locks that value when it lands. The 2% protocol fee comes off, and the net USD value is your entry weight. It isn't a ticket count, and your share of the total weight isn't the same as your chance of winning when ten prizes go to ten different wallets.

        What do winners get?

        A percentage of every asset in the vault, in kind. If the vault holds four kinds of token, a winner gets their percentage of each of the four. Nothing is swapped.

        Why is the payout split a draft?

        The reference split adds up to 96%. Where the remaining 4% goes hasn't been decided, so the page says so instead of quietly rounding it away.
